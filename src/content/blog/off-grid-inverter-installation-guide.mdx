A clean, safe installation is the difference between years of reliable
off-grid power and repeated service calls. This guide follows the sequence
our own engineers use when commissioning systems.

> **Safety first:** this guide is for qualified installers. Battery banks can
> deliver very high currents — a short circuit at the terminals is dangerous.
> Disconnect sources in the correct order and use insulated tools.

## Wiring order that avoids surprises

Connect in this order, and reverse it for disassembly:

1. **Grounding first** — connect the system ground (inverter chassis, panels
   and battery negative where the manual specifies) before any power wiring.
2. **Battery to inverter** — connect the battery bank with the correct fuse or
   breaker in line, observing polarity. Most inverters will not start until
   the DC input is present.
3. **PV array to the charge controller** — with panels covered or a breaker
   open, wire the array to the MPPT input.
4. **AC output** — connect the load side last, and keep it off until
   commissioning.

## Breaker and cable sizing

- **DC battery cable** — sized for the inverter's max current plus margin;
   too-small cable is the most common cause of voltage drop and heat.
- **Battery fuse/breaker** — rated above continuous current, placed at the
   battery terminal.
- **PV breaker** — rated for the string current, with the correct DC rating.
- **AC breaker** — matches the inverter's output rating and local code.

## Commissioning checklist

Before switching on:

- [ ] Polarity verified on battery and PV connections
- [ ] Torque on all power terminals per the manual
- [ ] Ground continuity confirmed
- [ ] Battery voltage within the inverter's input range
- [ ] No exposed conductors near terminals

Then power up in the same order as wiring: battery, then PV, then loads.
Watch the display for the first minutes — error codes or abnormal voltage
readings at this stage prevent much bigger problems later.

## Documentation matters

Record the wiring diagram, settings, and firmware version for each install.
This makes warranty and future expansion straightforward — and it is the
professional standard customers expect from a distributor's install team.
