Off-grid solar pricing varies widely by region, and prices move quickly, so
this guide focuses on the cost structure and the decisions that move the total
— not a fixed price list. Always get a current quotation for your market.

## What makes up the cost

| Component | Share of total | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery bank | 35–50% | The largest single cost — chemistry and capacity drive it |
| Solar panels | 20–30% | Cost per watt has fallen steadily |
| Inverter | 15–25% | Pure sine wave and surge capability matter |
| Wiring, breakers, mounting | 10–15% | Often underestimated |
| Installation & design | varies | Labor cost is highly regional |

## Typical system budgets

These are indicative ranges for hardware only, before installation:

| System | Daily energy | Indicative hardware budget |
|---|---|---|
| Small cabin / backup | 1–2 kWh/day | $1,500–3,000 |
| Home, partial loads | 3–5 kWh/day | $4,000–8,000 |
| Full off-grid home | 8–12 kWh/day | $10,000–20,000 |
| Farm / business | 15+ kWh/day | $20,000+ |

## The decisions that matter most

1. **Battery chemistry** — LiFePO4 costs more upfront but lasts 2–3× longer
   than lead-acid in cycles. Over a 10-year life, LiFePO4 is often cheaper.
2. **Array oversizing** — adding 20% more panels is cheap insurance that
   keeps batteries full in cloudy seasons.
3. **Inverter quality** — a pure sine wave unit with real surge capacity and
   certified testing protects the whole investment.
