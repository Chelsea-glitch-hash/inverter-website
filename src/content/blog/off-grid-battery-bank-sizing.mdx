Battery banks are the most expensive part of an off-grid system and the
easiest to get wrong. Size them too small and the system cuts out early on
cloudy days; too large and you pay for capacity you rarely use.

## The sizing formula

**Battery capacity (Ah) = daily energy (Wh) ÷ battery voltage (V) ÷ depth of discharge (%)**

Three variables drive the result:

1. **Daily energy use (Wh/day)** — from your load audit (see our
   [solar panel sizing guide](/blog/how-many-solar-panels-for-off-grid/))
2. **Battery voltage (V)** — 12 V, 24 V or 48 V, matching your inverter
3. **Depth of discharge (DoD)** — the usable portion of the battery

## Depth of discharge matters

Different chemistries allow different discharge depth:

| Battery type | Safe DoD | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lead-acid (flooded) | 50% | Regular topping charge needed |
| Lead-acid (AGM / gel) | 50–60% | Maintenance-free, moderate cost |
| LiFePO4 | 80–90% | Longer life, higher upfront cost |

## Worked example

Continuing the home from our [sizing guide](/blog/how-many-solar-panels-for-off-grid/):

- Daily load: **2,160 Wh**
- Inverter / system voltage: **48 V**
- Battery type: **LiFePO4** at 85% DoD
- Desired autonomy (no sun): **2 days**

**Step 1 — energy per day the battery must store:**
2,160 Wh ÷ 0.85 = **2,541 Wh of usable capacity**

**Step 2 — with 2 days of autonomy:**
2,541 × 2 = **5,082 Wh**

**Step 3 — capacity in amp-hours at 48 V:**
5,082 Wh ÷ 48 V = **106 Ah**

A practical choice: a **48 V, 100 Ah (≈ 5 kWh)** LiFePO4 bank, or two parallel
100 Ah packs if you want more margin.

## Matching the inverter

- Pick an inverter whose **DC input voltage** matches the bank (12/24/48 V).
- Keep the bank's charge current within the MPPT controller's rating — a
  5 kWh bank usually charges at 40–60 A comfortably.
- LiFePO4 banks need a BMS with communication (CAN/RS485) where the inverter
  supports it, so charging stops correctly.
