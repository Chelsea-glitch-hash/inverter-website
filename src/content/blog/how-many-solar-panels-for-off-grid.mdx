The single most common sizing mistake we see in off-grid projects is an
undersized solar array. Panels are cheaper than batteries and inverters to
replace later, so getting this number right matters more than any other part
of the design.

## Step 1: Find your daily energy need (Wh/day)

List every load, its power draw in watts, and the hours it runs per day.
Multiply and add: watts × hours = watt-hours per day.

**Worked example:**

| Load | Watts | Hours/day | Wh/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lights | 40 | 5 | 200 |
| Refrigerator | 150 | 8 | 1,200 |
| TV | 100 | 4 | 400 |
| Phone chargers | 15 | 4 | 60 |
| Water pump | 300 | 1 | 300 |
| **Total** | | | **2,160 Wh/day** |

## Step 2: Adjust for system losses

Off-grid systems lose energy to conversion, wiring and battery charging.
A conservative design multiplies the load by **1.3** (a 30% derating) to get
the energy the array must actually deliver:

**2,160 Wh × 1.3 = 2,808 Wh/day needed from PV**

## Step 3: Estimate your sun hours

"Peak sun hours" is the daily equivalent of full-strength sunlight for your
location. This is **not** the same as hours of daylight. Typical values:

- Northern Europe: 2.5–3.5
- Central Europe: 3.5–4.5
- Mediterranean: 4.5–5.5
- Middle East / North Africa: 5.5–6.5
- Southeast Asia / equatorial: 4.5–5.5
- US Southwest: 5.5–6.5

Use a conservative figure — the worst month, not the annual average, unless
you have a generator backup.

## Step 4: Calculate the array size

**Array watts = daily PV energy ÷ sun hours ÷ derating**

With 2,808 Wh needed and, say, 4.5 sun hours:

**2,808 ÷ 4.5 = 624 W minimum array**

We recommend adding a margin of 10–20% on top for cloudy streaks and panel
degradation, bringing the example to roughly **700–750 W** of panels.

## Step 5: Match the array to the inverter / controller

- Check the **MPPT operating voltage range** of your off-grid solar inverter
  and size the string voltage to sit in the middle of that range.
- Keep array power within the controller's maximum PV input rating.
- Wire string current below the controller's rated input current.

## Quick reference: panels per inverter

These figures assume ~550 W panels and 4.5 sun hours, with the 1.3 derating:

| Inverter size | Typical array (W) | Panels (~550 W) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5–2 kW | 900–1,200 | 2 |
| 3 kW | 1,500–1,800 | 3 |
| 5 kW | 2,400–3,000 | 5 |
| 8 kW | 3,600–4,500 | 7–8 |
