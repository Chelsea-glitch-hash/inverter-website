If you are a distributor, installer chain or brand owner sourcing inverters,
you will hear two terms: OEM and ODM. They describe different levels of
customization, and choosing the right model affects cost, speed and control
over your product.

## The two models

**OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer)** — you take an existing, proven
design and sell it under your brand. Customization is mostly cosmetic:
your logo, packaging, labels and manual. The hardware and firmware are the
manufacturer's standard product.

**ODM (Original Design Manufacturer)** — the manufacturer develops or
modifies the design to your specification: custom output configurations,
firmware parameters, communication protocols, even enclosure styling. You get
a product that is genuinely yours, at the cost of longer development.

## How the choice breaks down

| | OEM | ODM |
|---|---|---|
| Time to market | Fast | Slower (development + validation) |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Brand control | Logo + packaging | Full product identity |
| Technical differentiation | Little | Significant |
| MOQ | Lower | Higher |
| Best for | Quick private-label launches | Building a unique product line |

## What is customizable in practice

- **Private label** — logo, brand colors, carton, manual, display language
- **Firmware** — default parameters, communication protocols (CAN/RS485),
  grid-related settings for your region
- **Specifications** — output voltage (110/220 V), DC input ranges, socket
  types, and in some cases power ratings within the platform's limits
- **Quality & compliance** — certification sets for your destination market

## Choosing the right partner

Whatever model you choose, the manufacturer's factory matters more than the
label:

- Is the factory **real and inspectable**? Can you do a video audit?
- Is **testing documented** — functional test, aging test, traceability?
- Is **support available after the order** — spares, firmware updates, RMA?
