Correct sizing is what separates a hybrid system that delights the customer
from one that disappoints. Here is the five-step method our engineers use,
which you can apply directly to customer projects.

## Step 1: Audit the loads

List every load with its power draw and daily run hours. The result is a
daily energy budget in kWh. Don't guess — a data logger or the customer's
utility bills beat estimation every time.

**Example:** a household using 12 kWh/day with a 6 kW evening peak.

## Step 2: Size the PV array

Divide the daily budget by the local peak sun hours, then add 15–25% for
system losses, battery round-trip and panel degradation.

**Example:** 12 kWh ÷ 4.5 sun hours × 1.25 ≈ 3.3 kW → a 4 kW array
(8 × 500 W panels) leaves margin.

## Step 3: Size the battery bank

Decide the backup target: essential loads only (fridge, lights, router) or
the whole house. Multiply by the hours (or days) of autonomy required, and
respect the inverter's maximum charging current — a battery bank too small
to absorb PV output wastes generation.

**Example:** 8 kWh of storage (48 V × ~170 Ah usable) covers essential
loads overnight with one day of autonomy.

## Step 4: Select the inverter

The inverter's continuous rating must exceed the simultaneous peak load,
and its surge rating must cover motor starts. A 5 kW continuous /
10 kW surge unit — like our [HV-5000 hybrid inverter](/products/hybrid-inverters/hv-5000/) —
comfortably handles a 6 kW evening peak split across non-coincident loads
with inrush margin.

## Step 5: Check compliance

Confirm grid codes, export limits and certification requirements for the
destination market before quoting. This is where OEM support pays for
itself — we pre-configure market-specific firmware on the production line.
